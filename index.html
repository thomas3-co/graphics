Week 3 — Around the World with a Twist

Clear instructions, per-group rubric, and submission checklist for Class of 2027.

General Instructions for All Groups

Pick a subject from your group members and take a high-resolution portrait photo. Replace the background with your assigned city, retouch the face naturally, and redesign the pattern of cloth to reflect that city's unique culture and style identity. Your final image should tell a visual story that merges your subject's personality with the atmosphere of the city.

Class of 2027

Submission Format

1. Final Photoshop File (.PSD)

  • Include organized layers: background, subject, clothing, and retouching.
  • Rename layers clearly (e.g., "Clothing Edit", "Face Retouch").
  • Remove hidden or unnecessary layers for file cleanliness.

2. Final Image (.JPG or .PNG)

  • High resolution (minimum 2000 px wide).
  • Export at quality 10 or higher; avoid compression.
  • Filename format: Group#_CityName_ClassOf2027.jpg

3. Short Creative Reflection (PDF, max 1 page)

  • Title and one-paragraph concept summary.
  • Tools used: Pen Tool, Clone Stamp, Dodge & Burn, Gradient Map, etc.
  • Design choices: color palette, lighting, clothing influence, composition.
  • Include main challenges and lessons learned.

Creating a 3D Mockup and Composition

What: A 3D mockup is a digital visual showing your design applied on a realistic object like a phone, tablet, or poster. It helps visualize how your work looks in real life.

Why: It gives your design depth and realism. 3D mockups make presentations professional, help with perspective accuracy, and make your work more engaging.

When: After completing your main design (photo or layout), use Photoshop or an online tool like Photopea or Smartmockups to place your design inside a 3D frame or screen.

How:

  • Prepare your final image or layout.
  • Use a smart object or mockup template.
  • Insert your design into the mockup layer.
  • Adjust lighting, reflection, and perspective for realism.
  • Align background color or blur to match your theme.
  • Export the final composition as JPG or PNG.

Quick Tips

  • Use light and shadow to create depth.
  • Keep accents subtle but engaging.
  • Ensure clarity and composition.
  • Send your draft for subject-selection feedback.

Read Me — Detailed Notes

Step-by-step written lessons with tools, tips, and visual analogies. Each section can be downloaded on its own as a PDF. Opening a section counts toward your weekly reading check.

Introduction to Graphic Design & Photoshop

Graphic Design communicates ideas visually using typography, images, colors, and layout. Photoshop is a digital workspace where imagination becomes tangible.

  1. Open Photoshop and explore the workspace: Menu Bar, Toolbar, Panels, Options Bar.
  2. Familiarize yourself with essential panels: Layers, Adjustments, Properties, History.
  3. Learn non-destructive editing: use Layer Masks, Smart Objects, and Adjustment Layers.
  4. Practice: Draw simple shapes and apply color fills to understand layer stacking.
Analogy: Photoshop is like a designer's kitchen: brushes are spices, layers are ingredients, masks are containers, and smart objects are reusable recipes.
Tip: Always keep a "backup layer" for original images to prevent accidental mistakes.
Visual Example: Imagine a blank canvas as a plate. Each layer is a separate ingredient. Masks are lids that cover parts of layers without deleting them. Smart Objects are frozen ingredients you can use later without changing the original taste.

Raster vs Vector Graphics

Raster images are pixel-based, perfect for detailed photos. Vector graphics are path-based and scalable without losing quality.

  • Raster: Photos, textures, paintings.
  • Vector: Logos, icons, illustrations requiring scalability.
  1. Open a raster image, zoom in — notice pixels when enlarged.
  2. Draw a shape with the Pen Tool — crisp at any zoom.
  3. Save raster as JPG/PNG, vector as SVG/AI.
  4. Practice: Trace a raster logo into a vector path.
Pen Tool: Precise path creation.
Analogy: Tracing a logo on tracing paper for clean outlines.
Pro Tip: Combine raster & vector: vector logos with raster textures for realism.
Visual Example: Imagine a photo of a cat (raster) vs a cartoon cat outline (vector). Zoom the photo: pixels appear. Zoom the cartoon: it stays sharp, clean, and scalable.

Photopea Overview

Photopea is an online Photoshop alternative supporting layers, masks, smart objects, and adjustments.

  1. Visit photopea.com.
  2. Open or drag an image.
  3. Use Move, Brush, Clone Stamp, and Adjustment Layers.
  4. Save as PSD for future edits or export as PNG/JPG.
  5. Practice: Open a Photoshop PSD in Photopea and observe layer compatibility.
Analogy: Photopea is a pocket Photoshop — light and ready anywhere.
Tip: Learn Photopea shortcuts; most match Photoshop for faster workflow.
Visual Example: Picture carrying a small, foldable art studio in your laptop browser, with all brushes, layers, and panels ready.

Removing Backgrounds and Subjects

Essential for product design, e-commerce, social media, and composites.

  1. Select the subject using Quick Selection Tool or Select Subject.
  2. Refine edges using Select and Mask.
  3. Apply Layer Mask to hide the background non-destructively.
  4. Use Refine Edge Brush for hair or intricate details.
Analogy: Digital scissors that are reversible.
Tip: Check edges at 200% zoom for perfection.
Visual Example: Imagine cutting a portrait out of a magazine but having invisible scissors that allow you to adjust edges after cutting.

Passport Size Photo

Precise cropping, clean background, and correct exposure are key.

  1. Crop to 2x2 inches using Crop Tool.
  2. Adjust background with Solid Color Fill or Brush Tool.
  3. Dodge & Burn to balance skin tone.
  4. Save at 300 dpi.
Analogy: Trimming a printed photo to fit exactly into a passport window.
Tip: Use soft brush edges for smooth background transitions.
Visual Example: Picture a perfectly framed passport headshot on a white canvas, with soft shadows blending into the background.

Face and Skin Retouching

Enhance portraits subtly while preserving texture.

  1. Duplicate the original layer.
  2. Spot Healing Brush for blemishes.
  3. Frequency Separation: separate color & texture for precise edits.
  4. Dodge & Burn for highlights and contours.
Healing Brush: Digital makeup without destroying skin texture.
Tip: Zoom at 100–200% to see true texture.
Visual Example: Imagine painting small corrections over a face while keeping pores and skin details intact.

Teeth and Eye Whitening, Reflection & Correction

Brighten eyes and teeth subtly while maintaining realism.

  1. Dodge Tool 10–15% exposure for brightening.
  2. Add reflections with Overlay layers.
  3. Liquify Tool for small alignment fixes.
Liquify Tool: Gentle reshaping like soft clay.
Tip: Keep whitening subtle to avoid a fake look.
Visual Example: Visualize adding a soft highlight to the eyes like a sunbeam, without changing natural color.

Posture Correction & Dress Mockups

Adjust posture and garments for realistic presentation using Smart Objects.

  1. Puppet Warp for subtle posture corrections.
  2. Convert garments to Smart Objects.
  3. Warp Transform for folds and fabric flow.
  4. Adjust shadows and highlights for realism.
Puppet Warp: Controlled string movement like a puppet.
Tip: Make small incremental adjustments to avoid unnatural distortion.
Visual Example: Picture moving an arm slightly, like bending a paper doll, and the clothes following naturally with realistic folds.

Tool Reuse & Project Application

Skills compound — mastering one tool allows multiple creative applications.

  • Healing Brush: Polishing portraits & products.
  • Pen Tool: Clean logos & scalable shapes.
  • Liquify: Fashion edits & symmetry correction.
  • Smart Objects: Reusable templates, non-destructive scaling.
Tip: Always name layers & groups. Reusable workflows depend on organization.
Visual Example: Imagine a toolbox where each tool can be picked and reused in multiple projects — nothing is wasted, everything is reusable.
